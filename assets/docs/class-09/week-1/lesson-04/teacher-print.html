Teacher guide · Mouse, keyboard, typing and shortcuts

Computer Literacy & Workplace Digital Skills

Class: Class 09   Level: Beginner   Duration: 150-180 minutes

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate core mouse actions safely.
  • Use common keys and shortcuts to enter and edit text.
  • Type and proofread a short workplace message with comfortable posture.

Materials

Windows computer per learner or pair, mouse or touchpad, keyboard, sample text, projector, reading and practice worksheet.

Lesson sequence

TimeTeaching and learning activity
0-10Retrieval: recall desktop and taskbar parts from Lesson 3.
10-30Demonstrate point, click, double-click, right-click, drag and scroll. Learners mirror each action.
30-45Explore pointer shapes and touchpad alternatives. Gestures vary by device.
45-65Keyboard tour: editing keys, modifiers, navigation and function keys. Model Shift and Caps Lock.
65-80Typing posture check and short accuracy exercise. Give feedback on accuracy before speed.
80-90Break and eye rest.
90-110Model copy, cut, paste, undo, select all, save, switch windows and lock shortcuts.
110-130Guided practice and observation. Ask learners to explain each action.
130-140Self-marking quiz. Target 8/10; review missed items.
140-150Explain quick-guide assignment and collect exit check.
150-180Optional guided extension: extra keyboard practice, individual coaching and quick-guide feedback.

Formative assessment: observe, prompt, retry

Use this guide during practice. Mark I for independent, P for completes after a prompt, or R for revisit after another demonstration. This is for feedback, not grading.

Teacher prompt: What are you trying to do, and what on the screen tells you the action worked? Give one small hint, then let the learner try again.

Quiz answer key

1 B; 2 C; 3 A; 4 B; 5 B; 6 C; 7 B; 8 A; 9 B; 10 C. Review mouse selection, Backspace, Shift, copy, save, window switching, proofreading and typing comfort when learners miss these items.

Differentiation

  • Pair learners and rotate driver/coach roles.
  • Offer printed reading and paper responses when devices are limited.
  • Extension: select a sentence and apply copy, paste and undo.
  • Allow additional time and larger text or accessibility settings.

Common misconceptions

  • Click and double-click are interchangeable: clarify select versus open.
  • Backspace and Delete behave identically: show cursor position and direction.
  • Cut and copy both leave the original: cut is intended to move selection.
  • Speed is the only measure: accuracy, comfort and proofreading matter.
