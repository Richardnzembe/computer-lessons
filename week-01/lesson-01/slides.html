The big idea

What is a computer?

An electronic machine that accepts data, processes it using instructions, produces information, and can store it for later.

  • It follows a program — it does not think for itself.
  • It works with data: numbers, words, images and sound.
  • It can repeat the same task quickly and accurately.
The shape does not define the computer. The four jobs do.

Clearing up a myth

The monitor is not the computer

  • The screen only shows output. The computer may be under the desk, inside the screen, or in your hand.
  • A desktop separates the case, monitor, keyboard and mouse.
  • A laptop combines them in one foldable case.
  • A phone is a complete computer that also makes calls.
  • An embedded computer is hidden inside another machine.
Ask yourself: where are the four jobs happening? That is where the computer is.

The information cycle

Every computer does four jobs

InputData goes in — keyboard, mouse, touch, voice, scanner.
ProcessingThe processor follows instructions: calculate, sort, check.
OutputThe result appears on screen, paper or speakers.
StorageThe result is kept for later, even after power off.

Why it matters

Why employers expect computer skills

  • Accurate records — stock, sales, attendance, patients, customers.
  • Faster reporting — totals and summaries in minutes, not hours.
  • Professional communication — clear email and documents.
  • Research and planning — finding reliable information online.
  • Confidentiality — protecting personal and business data.
Computers support good judgement. They do not replace it.

Types of computers

Different shapes, same four jobs

DesktopFixed desk workstation; best value for screen and power.
LaptopPortable all-in-one; full keyboard and screen.
TabletTouch-first; good for counters, forms and reading.
SmartphonePocket computer; communication on the move.

Pocket and touch

Smartphones and tablets are computers

Smartphone A pocket computer with a mobile network.
  • Email, maps, mobile money, photos
  • Great for quick communication
  • Awkward for long reports and spreadsheets
Tablet A larger touchscreen computer.
  • Counters, field forms, reading
  • Simple to use with a finger
  • Slower for heavy typing; fewer ports
Do not judge a computer by its size. Judge it by the job it can do.

High-performance

When the work is demanding: workstations

A workstation is a high-performance desktop built for professional tasks that need sustained processing, memory and graphics power.

  • Video editing and animation
  • Graphic design and engineering
  • Large data analysis and CAD
Expensive, fixed, and usually paired with specialised software.
Typical user Video editor, designer, engineer, data analyst. Not for Basic typing, email and light browsing — a normal desktop is enough.

Shared computing

One computer, many users: servers

A server provides files, email, websites or databases to other computers over a network.

File serverShared folders for staff documents and records.
Email serverStores and delivers company email.
Web serverHosts websites and online services.
Servers need backups, security and an administrator. They are not used at a desk.

Hidden computers

Computers inside everyday machines

An embedded computer is built inside another machine to control it. It has one dedicated job.

Washing machineControls water level, temperature and spin.
CarManages engine timing, brakes and sensors.
ATMVerifies cards and processes transactions.
Traffic lightTimes the sequence of signals.
Clinic deviceMeasures and displays a patient reading.
Smart TVStreams apps and processes video.

Specialist

Very large systems: mainframes

A mainframe is a very large, powerful computer that handles enormous numbers of transactions at the same time.

  • Banks — millions of account transactions
  • Airlines — bookings, schedules and payments
  • Government — national records and services
They live in secure data centres and are managed by specialists. You will meet their services through a normal desktop or phone.

Key takeaways

What to remember

  • A computer accepts data, processes it, produces information and stores it.
  • Desktop, laptop, tablet, phone, workstation, server, embedded and mainframe are all computers.
  • Choose a device by the task, location, users and budget — not by size or price alone.
  • Computers support accurate records, clear communication and faster decisions.
