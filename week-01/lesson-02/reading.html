Computer hardware, software & devices

A computer is a partnership: physical parts that do the work, and programs that tell them what to do. This reading opens the case, identifies each component, connects the peripherals and explains the software that brings the machine to life.

Reading progress

In Lesson 1 you learned that every computer performs four jobs: input, processing, output and storage. This lesson asks a more practical question: which part actually does each job, and what tells it to? The answer is hardware and software working together.

Section 01Hardware and software

Hardware is every physical part of a computer that you can see and touch: the case, screen, keyboard, cables, processor, memory and storage. Software is the set of programs and instructions that tells the hardware what to do. Software has no physical shape; it is stored as files on the hardware.

Key idea

Hardware sets the limits — how fast, how much and how large. Software decides what actually happens within those limits. A powerful computer with poor software is frustrating; good software on weak hardware is slow. You need both.

Think of a kitchen. Pots, knives, an oven and a fridge are the hardware. Recipes are the software: they tell the equipment what to do and in what order. Buying a bigger oven does not help if the recipe is wrong, and a perfect recipe cannot succeed without a working oven.

Examples of hardware and software
Hardware — you can touch itSoftware — you cannot touch it
Monitor, keyboard, mouseWindows 11, macOS, Linux, Android
Processor, memory, hard drive or SSDMicrosoft Word, Excel, PowerPoint
Printer, scanner, speakers, webcamGoogle Chrome, WhatsApp, email apps
USB flash drive, memory card, routerAntivirus, backup tools, device drivers

Hardware is often divided into internal components inside the case and peripherals connected outside it. Software is divided into system software, which runs the computer, and application software, which helps you do a task. Both divisions are covered below.

Section 02Inside the case

Desktop computers separate the working parts from the screen and keyboard. A laptop packs the same parts into a thin case, and a phone packs them into your hand. The names and jobs stay the same, even when the size changes.

Processor (CPU) The brain

Carries out the instructions of a program: calculating, comparing, sorting and moving data.

Look for: speed in GHz, number of cores, generation.If it is weak: everything feels slow, especially heavy programs.

Memory (RAM) Working space

Temporary working memory that holds open programs and the data they are using right now.

Look for: 8 GB for office work; 16 GB or more for heavy tasks.If it is small: the computer struggles with many tabs or large files.

Storage Filing cabinet

Keeps files, programs and the operating system even when the power is off.

Look for: SSD for speed; 256 GB minimum, 512 GB comfortable.If it is full: updates fail and the system slows down.

Graphics card (GPU) Images

Processes images and video and sends the picture to the monitor.

Look for: integrated graphics for office work; dedicated GPU for design and video.If it is weak: video editing, games and design software stutter.

Power supply (PSU) Energy

Converts mains electricity into the steady low voltages the components need.

Look for: enough wattage for the components; a surge protector.If it fails: the computer will not start, or restarts unexpectedly.

Motherboard Backbone

The main circuit board that connects the processor, memory, storage, ports and power.

Look for: the right sockets and ports for the parts you need.If it fails: the machine may not start or devices stop being recognised.

Two more parts matter in practice. The cooling system — a heatsink and fan, or liquid cooling — keeps the processor from overheating. The case holds and protects everything and guides air through the machine. Dust, blocked vents and high room temperatures cause overheating, which slows the computer and shortens its life.

Memory is not storage

In everyday speech people say “memory” for both, but they are different. RAM is temporary working space and is cleared when the power goes off. Storage — an SSD, hard drive or flash drive — keeps files until you delete them. A computer with 8 GB of RAM and a 256 GB SSD has two very different numbers.

QuestionRAMStorage
What does it hold?Open programs and the data in useFiles, programs and the operating system
Kept when the power is off?No — contents are lostYes — until deleted

Effect of too littleSlow switching, freezing, “out of memory”Cannot save files or install updates

Section 03Devices, ports and cables

A peripheral is a device connected to the computer to add a function: typing, pointing, printing, scanning, sound, pictures or extra storage. Peripherals are classified by the direction the data travels.

DirectionWhat it doesExamples
InputSends data or commands into the computerKeyboard, mouse, touchpad, microphone, webcam, scanner, barcode reader, touchscreen
OutputPresents the result to the userMonitor, projector, printer, plotter, speakers, headphones
BothAccepts input and produces outputTouchscreen, headset with microphone, multifunction printer-scanner, external drive

Most peripherals connect through a port. A port is a socket on the computer, and a connector is the plug on the cable. Knowing the common ports saves time and prevents damage.

PortUsually connectsNotes
USB-AKeyboard, mouse, flash drive, printerThe rectangular USB found on most desktops and older laptops.
USB-CChargers, drives, docks, some monitorsSmall, reversible and fast. Not every USB-C port carries video or power.
HDMIMonitor, projector, TVCarries both video and sound. The usual choice for a second screen.
DisplayPortMonitorsCommon on desktop graphics cards; also carries video and sound.
Ethernet (RJ-45)Wired networkFaster and more stable than Wi-Fi when a cable can reach.
3.5 mm audioHeadphones, microphone, speakersRound jack; often separate green (output) and pink (input) sockets.
PowerCharger or mains cableDifferent shapes for different models — never force a plug.

Not every connection needs a cable. Wi-Fi connects to a network, Bluetooth connects nearby devices such as mice, keyboards and headsets, and mobile data connects a phone or tablet to the internet. Wireless is convenient, but a cable is usually faster and more reliable for heavy work.

Workplace tip

Label cables at shared desks and keep them tidy. A loose or forced connector is one of the most common causes of “the printer is not working” and “the monitor has no signal”. Before calling for help, check that the right cable is in the right port at both ends.

Section 04Software and the operating system

Software is the instructions. It is convenient to group it into three kinds:

KindPurposeExamples
Operating systemStarts the computer, manages hardware, files, users and programsWindows 10/11, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS
ApplicationsHelp the user complete a taskWord, Excel, PowerPoint, Chrome, email, WhatsApp, accounting software
UtilitiesMaintain, protect or repair the computerAntivirus, backup, disk cleanup, file compression, updates

The operating system (OS) is the most important software on the machine. Without it, the hardware cannot start, the screen stays blank, and no application can run. Its main jobs are:

  • Starting up — checking the hardware and loading the system.
  • Managing hardware — talking to the screen, keyboard, printer and network through drivers.
  • Managing memory and processing — deciding which program uses which resources.
  • Managing files and storage — creating folders, copying, moving, renaming and deleting files.
  • Providing the interface — the desktop, Start menu, taskbar, windows and dialogs.
  • Managing users and security — sign-in, permissions, updates and protection.

Try it now

Open a document and type a sentence. Which application are you using? Which operating system is underneath it? Where will the file live when you save it — RAM or storage?

Files carry an extension — the letters after the dot — that tells the operating system which application should open them. Common examples are .docx for Word, .xlsx for Excel, .pptx for PowerPoint, .pdf for a fixed document, .jpg for a photo and .mp3 for audio. You will work with these in Week 2.

Common mistake

“More software means a better computer.” Not true. Unused programs take up storage, and software from unknown sources can carry malware. Install only what the work needs, keep it updated, and use one trusted antivirus rather than several.

Section 05Reading a specification and caring for equipment

When you buy or compare computers, you will see a short specification. You do not need to memorise every number, but you should be able to read the important ones and connect them to the work.

SpecificationWhat it meansWhy it matters
Intel Core i5 (12th gen)The processor family and generationNewer generations are usually faster and more efficient
16 GB RAMAmount of working memoryMore open programs, tabs and large files at once
512 GB SSDSolid-state storage and capacityFast startup and loading, with room for files
15.6-inch Full HD displayScreen size and resolutionComfort and detail for long documents and images
Windows 11 HomeThe operating systemDetermines the interface and which software runs
Integrated graphicsGraphics built into the processorFine for office work; not for demanding video or design

Choose by the work, not by the label. A receptionist needs a reliable desktop, a comfortable keyboard and a good screen. An accountant needs memory and a second monitor for large spreadsheets. A designer needs a strong processor, a dedicated graphics card and plenty of RAM. A field officer needs a light laptop with good battery life.

Good care keeps equipment working and protects the organisation’s data:

  • Use a surge protector and shut down properly instead of switching off at the wall.
  • Keep vents clear and clean dust from fans; never use a computer on a soft surface that blocks airflow.
  • Keep liquids away and never force a connector into a port.
  • Back up important files to an external drive or cloud storage, and test that the backup opens.
  • Install updates and use strong passwords; lock the screen when you leave your desk.
  • Set up the workstation so the screen is at eye level, the keyboard is flat and the chair supports your back.

Workplace tip

An SSD is often the cheapest way to make an older office computer feel new: startup, file opening and saving all speed up. More RAM helps when many programs run at once. Neither will fix a slow internet connection or a virus.

Section 06Key terms to remember

These words will come back throughout the programme. Copy them into your notebook and write one example of your own for each.

Hardware
The physical parts of a computer that you can touch.
Software
The programs and instructions that tell the hardware what to do.
Peripheral
A device connected to the computer to add a function, such as a printer or scanner.
Processor (CPU)
The component that carries out instructions and calculations.
RAM
Temporary working memory that holds open programs and data while the computer is on.
Storage
Keeps files and programs when the power is off, on an SSD, hard drive or flash drive.
SSD / HDD
Solid-state drive (fast, no moving parts) and hard disk drive (larger and cheaper, with moving parts).
Graphics card (GPU)
The component that processes images and video for the display.
Motherboard
The main circuit board that connects the components.
Power supply (PSU)
Converts mains electricity into the power the components need.
Port
A socket where a cable or device connects to the computer.
USB / HDMI
Common connection standards: USB for peripherals, HDMI for monitors and projectors.
Operating system
The system software that starts the computer and manages hardware, files and programs.
Driver
A small program that lets the operating system communicate with a device.
Application
Software that helps the user complete a task, such as Word or Excel.
Utility
Software that maintains, protects or repairs the computer, such as antivirus or backup.
File extension
The letters after the dot that show a file’s type, such as .docx or .pdf.
Backup
A copy of files kept in another place so work can be restored if something fails.

Section 07Check your understanding

Answer each question in your notebook first, then open the answer to check yourself.

1. What is the difference between hardware and software? Give one example of each.
Hardware is the physical equipment you can touch — for example a monitor or a keyboard. Software is the set of instructions that tells the hardware what to do — for example Windows 11 or Microsoft Word. Neither is useful without the other.
2. Why does a saved document survive when the power goes off, but an open unsaved document does not?
The open document is in RAM, which is temporary working memory and is cleared when the power goes off. Once you save it, the file is written to storage — an SSD, hard drive or cloud drive — which keeps it until you delete it.
3. Name three jobs of an operating system.
Any three of: starting the computer; managing hardware through drivers; managing memory and processing; organising files and storage; providing the desktop and windows; managing users, permissions and security.
4. Which port would you use for a monitor, and which for a wired network connection?
A monitor usually connects with HDMI or DisplayPort; a wired network uses Ethernet (RJ-45). USB-C may also carry video on some computers, but you should check that the port supports it.
5. A colleague says, “My computer is slow, so I will add more RAM.” When would that help, and when would it not?
More RAM helps when many programs, browser tabs or large files are open at once and the computer is constantly swapping data to storage. It will not fix a slow internet connection, a full or failing storage drive, malware, overheating or an old processor. Diagnose the cause before buying an upgrade.

Section 08Lesson summary

  • Hardware is the physical equipment; software is the instructions that make it work.
  • The core internal parts are the processor, RAM, storage, graphics card, power supply and motherboard, kept cool by fans and heatsinks.
  • RAM is temporary working memory; storage keeps files when the power is off.
  • Peripherals are input, output or both, and connect through ports such as USB, HDMI, DisplayPort, Ethernet and audio, or wirelessly.
  • The operating system starts the computer and manages hardware, files, programs and users; applications help you do tasks and utilities protect and maintain the machine.
  • Choose equipment by the work, software, location, users and budget, then protect it with backups, updates and sensible care.

Before you move on

You should be able to name the main components, explain the difference between RAM and storage, and describe what an operating system does. If you cannot yet, re-read Sections 2 and 4 before starting the slides.
