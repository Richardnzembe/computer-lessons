Windows Desktop Setup & Navigation Guide

Your third workplace task. Prove you can start up, sign in and move around Windows safely — then turn those skills into a one-page first-day guide a new colleague could follow.

30 marks · Computer Fundamentals
Week 1 · Day 3 · Practical assignment

Dalswin Business Centre

Simulated office task · Prepare a desktop and navigation guide for a new starter

30marks
SetWeek 1 · Day 3
DueEnd of Day 3
FormatWord document, PDF or paper
SubmitStudent portal or instructor
Your role: You have joined Dalswin Business Centre as an office assistant. Before new staff are given access, the office manager wants proof that you can start up and use a Windows computer safely. She also wants a short guide you can hand to the next new starter. Every action below must be performed on a real Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer. Windows 10 is acceptable — just note in your guide where it differs from Windows 11, such as the Start menu position.

1Navigation check 8 marks

Perform each action below on the computer you can access. For each one, record exactly how you did it — the keys, the menu or the button you used. Tick the box only when the action actually worked.

#ActionSteps or keys you usedDone
1Start the computer and sign in to your own account
2Open the Start menu
3Find an app using Search (without scrolling the app list)
4Open two different apps at the same time
5Place the two windows side by side
6Switch between the two windows without using the mouse
7Minimise one window, then bring it back
8Lock the screen, then unlock it

If your computer is shared, ask before signing in or changing settings. Never use another person’s account.

2Desktop map 8 marks

Complete the table below. For each part, say where on the screen you find it and one job it does. Use plain words a new starter would understand.

Part of the desktopWhere you find itOne job it does
Start button
Search box

Recycle Bin

Show desktop

3Everyday actions 8 marks

For each situation, write the action you would take and any keys or shortcuts. There may be more than one correct answer — mark the one you would actually use.

SituationWhat you would doKeys or shortcut
It is the start of your shift and the computer is off.
You cannot remember where a program is installed.
You need to read a document while typing an email.
You must leave your desk for ten minutes.
A colleague needs the computer for the afternoon.
A program has stopped responding and will not close.
The screen is too bright for the room.
You have finished for the day and the computer stays on the desk.

Remember the difference: lock keeps your programs open, sign out closes your session, and shut down switches the computer off properly.

4First-day guide and submission 6 marks

Now turn your work into a tidy one-page first-day guide for a new colleague. It must be written in short, clear steps — imagine the reader has never used this computer before. Your guide must include:

  • starting up and signing in — what happens after you press the power button;
  • a labelled desktop map — the taskbar, Start button, Search and notification area marked on a simple sketch or screenshot;
  • finding and opening an app — the fastest method plus one alternative;
  • working with windows — minimise, maximise, snap side by side and Alt + Tab;
  • leaving the computer safely — when to lock, sign out or shut down;
  • one safety or comfort tip from the reading, such as the 20-20-20 rule.

Save the file as W1D3_Desktop_Guide_YourName. Keep it in your Work Documents folder — the Week 1 assessment will ask you to reuse the folder.
