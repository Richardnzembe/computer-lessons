Answer Key — Quiz & Practice Lab

Model answers for the ten-question quiz, the 20-item practice worksheet and the five reading check questions, with brief marking guidance.

Quiz: 10 marksPractice: 20 marksReading: 5 checksNot for learners
Instructor copy. Do not share this document with learners before they attempt the quiz or worksheet. Accept any answer that carries the same meaning as the model answer.

1Quiz — ten questions

QAnswerCorrect option and explanation
1AStart → Power → Shut down. A proper shutdown closes programs and saves settings. Holding the power button or cutting the power can lose work.
2BWindows key + L. It locks without closing programs. Windows + D shows the desktop, Ctrl + L is used in browsers, Alt + F4 closes a window.
3CSigning out closes your session and programs; locking keeps them open. Lock is for a short absence; sign out is for handing the computer to another person.
4BApps, settings and power options in one menu. The Start menu is the main entrance to Windows.
5DThe Search box on the taskbar. Search finds apps, settings and files as you type.
6BThe notification area. It sits at the right of the taskbar and holds the clock, network, volume and battery icons.
7CMakes the window fill the screen. Minimise hides the window; Close ends the program.
8DWindows key + ←. Windows + → snaps to the right. Together they place two windows side by side.
9CSnap layouts. Hover over the Maximise button in Windows 11 to choose a grid of windows.
10DClose the program with the X or Task Manager. Wait a moment, then close it; use Ctrl + Shift + Esc if needed. Cutting power is a last resort.

Marking: one mark per correct answer. Target: 8/10. Learners below 6/10 should review the reading and retry before starting the assignment.

What should you do? One mark per item.

#AnswerSituation and reason
1Sign inStart of shift — sign in with your own account so your files, settings and permissions are available.
2LockTen-minute meeting — lock (Windows key + L) so work stays open but nobody can see or change it.
3Shut downEnd of day and power off at the wall — shut down from Start → Power so programs close safely first.
4Sign outColleague needs the computer — signing out closes your session so they can sign in under their own account.
5SleepLunch — sleep saves power and restores open programs when you wake the computer.
6RestartComputer frozen — a restart clears the stuck state. Close the frozen program first if possible.

5Reading check — suggested answers

1Between the power button and the desktop. The firmware runs the POST and tests the hardware; the boot loader finds Windows on the drive; Windows loads its core and drivers; the sign-in screen appears; after sign-in, Windows loads your personal settings and shows the desktop.
2Lock or sign out? Lock when you are stepping away briefly and want to keep your programs open. Sign out when another person will use the same computer under their own account, because signing out ends your session and closes your programs.
3Fastest way to open a program. Press the Windows key and type the program’s name, then press Enter. Search also finds settings, files and web results.
4Side by side, and switching. Use Windows + ← and Windows + → to snap the windows to each half, or drag a window to the left or right edge. Switch with Alt + Tab, click the taskbar buttons, or open Task View with Windows + Tab.
5Two display settings and two safety habits. Display: brightness, scale, resolution, Night light, or the arrangement of a second monitor. Safety: lock the screen when leaving the desk, shut down properly, install updates, and keep your password private.
