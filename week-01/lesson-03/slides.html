Start-up

From the power button to the desktop

1 · Power onThe power supply starts the firmware.
2 · Hardware checkThe POST tests memory, storage and devices.
3 · Windows loadsThe OS starts its core and drivers.
4 · Sign inPassword, PIN, fingerprint or face.
5 · DesktopYour settings, icons and taskbar appear.
Firmware → boot loader → Windows → sign-in → desktop.

Identity

Signing in tells Windows who you are

Your account unlocks your files, settings and permissions. On a shared computer, every person signs in separately.

  • Password — protects the account, especially online.
  • PIN — a short code tied to this one device.
  • Windows Hello — fingerprint or face recognition on supported machines.
Never share your password — and never leave a signed-in computer unattended.
At a shared desk Sign in with your own account. When you finish, sign out so the next person starts clean. Privacy Lock the screen with Windows + L before you walk away.

The desktop

Your main work area

The desktop is the background layer you see after signing in. Windows open on top of it.

  • Wallpaper — the background picture.
  • Icons — shortcuts to programs, folders and files.
  • Taskbar — Start, Search, apps and status.
  • Recycle Bin — deleted files waiting to be restored or removed.
Right-click the desktop for View, Sort by and Display settings.
Wallpaper + icons The picture is decoration; the icons are your shortcuts. Before you customise On a work computer, keep the desktop tidy and avoid personal pictures in client-facing settings.

Search

Type three letters, press Enter

Search looks through apps, settings and files as you type. It is usually faster than hunting through menus.

  • Press Windows and type calc → Calculator.
  • Type wifi → Wi-Fi settings.
  • Type part of a file name → recent documents.
  • Press Esc to close the search.
If you do not know the name, browse All apps or use Settings.
Workplace habit Search by name instead of clicking through folders. It is quicker and less error-prone. Careful Web results can include adverts and downloads. Only open sources you trust.

Windows

Every window has the same frame

  • Title bar — the app and document name.
  • Control buttons — minimise, maximise, close.
  • Toolbar or ribbon — the commands for the app.
  • Work area — the document, sheet or canvas.
  • Scroll bar — move through content that does not fit.
  • Status bar — information at the bottom, such as zoom or page count.
Drag the title bar to move a window; drag an edge to resize it.
Title bar App name on the left, three control buttons on the right. Work area Where the actual document or image lives.

Snap

Two windows, side by side

Snap places a window against a screen edge so it fills half or a quarter of the screen.

  • Windows + ← — snap left.
  • Windows + → — snap right.
  • Drag to an edge — snap with the mouse.
  • Windows 11: hover the Maximise button for Snap layouts.
Snap two apps, then click a taskbar button to swap which one is active.
Real work Spreadsheet on the left, invoice on the right. No endless clicking between full-screen windows.

Comfort and safety

Work comfortably, work safely

  • Screen at eye level and about an arm’s length away.
  • Reduce glare; use Night light in the evening.
  • 20-20-20 — every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  • Wrists flat, feet supported; stand and stretch each hour.
  • Lock the screen with Windows + L when you leave.
  • Install updates and restart so they finish.
A tidy desk and an updated computer protect both your health and the organisation’s data.
