Teacher guide: mouse, keyboard, typing & shortcuts

Week 1 · Lesson 4 · Class 09 · Beginner level

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate core mouse actions safely.
  • Use common keys and shortcuts to enter and edit text.
  • Type and proofread a short workplace message with comfortable posture.

Materials

Windows computer per learner or pair, mouse or touchpad, keyboard, sample text, projector, reading and practice worksheet.

150-minute lesson sequence

TimeTeaching and learning activity
0?10Retrieval: ask learners to recall desktop and taskbar parts from Lesson 3.
10?30Demonstrate point, click, double-click, right-click, drag and scroll. Learners mirror each action.
30?45Explore pointer shapes and touchpad alternatives. Clarify that gestures can vary.
45?65Keyboard tour: editing keys, modifiers, navigation and function keys. Model Shift and Caps Lock.
65?80Typing posture check and short accuracy exercise. Give feedback on accuracy before speed.
80?90Break and eye rest.
90?110Model copy, cut, paste, undo, select all, save, switch windows and lock shortcuts.
110?130Guided practice lab and observation. Ask learners to explain what each action does.
130?140Self-marking quiz. Target: 8/10; review missed items.
140?150Explain quick-guide assignment and collect exit check: one mouse action, key and shortcut.

Assessment and key

Formative evidence: accurate mouse selection, deliberate text entry, appropriate shortcut choice and proofreading. Quiz answers: 1 B, 2 C, 3 A, 4 B, 5 B, 6 C, 7 B, 8 A, 9 B, 10 C.

Differentiation

  • Pair learners and rotate the driver/coach roles for device sharing.
  • Offer printed reading and paper responses where access is limited.
  • For extension, let confident learners demonstrate selecting a sentence and applying copy/paste/undo.
  • Allow extra time and larger text or accessibility settings where needed.

Common misconceptions

  • Click and double-click are interchangeable: clarify select versus open.
  • Backspace and Delete behave identically: show the cursor position and direction.
  • Cut and copy both leave the original: cut is intended to move the selection.
  • Speed is the only measure of typing: accuracy, comfort and proofreading matter.
